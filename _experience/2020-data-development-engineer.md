I joined Shantui in 2020. Much of my day involved SQL, spreadsheets, and the familiar message: “Could you help me check this number?” Sometimes it was a quick query. Sometimes the same figure appeared differently in two reports, and finding out why took most of the afternoon.

### What the work looked like

I helped pull together production and operational records, maintain regular reports, and prepare charts for colleagues who needed to see what had changed. There were data checks, small fixes to reporting scripts, and requests for a new filter or an extra column. I also spent time tracing missing records and working out whether an unusual result came from the source data or from the query.

One recurring job was reconciling numbers across reports. Before changing any code, I learned to check the date range, status definitions, and whether the report counted records or distinct items. Quite often, the explanation was less dramatic than the first message suggested.

Not every request arrived fully formed. I would ask who needed the report, how often they used it, and what they wanted to check. A rough first version was usually a better starting point for the conversation than trying to guess every requirement in advance. Once something worked, I tried to leave enough notes that the next person would not have to start from zero.

I liked the small satisfactions: a routine report running without another manual fix, a colleague finding the information they needed, or finally spotting the filter that had been causing a mismatch. There were also slow afternoons and urgent requests just as I thought I was finished for the day. It was ordinary work, with ordinary frustrations.

Over those years I became more comfortable saying, “I haven't found the cause yet, but this is what I've checked.” I got better at keeping people updated, asking a clear question, and not taking every change request personally. I also learned a fair amount from colleagues who knew the business far better than I did. Some of the most useful conversations happened over lunch, away from the spreadsheet that had started them.
