Being a teaching assistant is a mixture of explaining things and making sure the everyday arrangements work. I help prepare materials and exercises, check that example code runs, organize coursework, and answer questions before or after class. Occasionally, the most pressing problem is simply that a file will not open on someone's laptop.

The tasks change with the week. Before a class, I might work through an exercise and flag a step that needs a little more explanation. Around a submission deadline, there are more questions about data, software, or why an output looks different from the example. I also help students organize practical projects and read their results alongside the question they originally wanted to answer.

A few things come up repeatedly:

- checking a student's code together, often starting with the file path or the column names;
- working through a small numerical example when a formula feels too abstract;
- collecting common questions so the instructor can revisit them in class;
- helping students make a report readable, including labels, units, and an explanation of what the figures show.

I sometimes need to go away and check an answer myself. I would rather tell a student that and follow up later than give a confident explanation that turns out to be wrong. There are also days when an explanation that worked for one person does nothing for the next, and we have to try a different route.

The pleasant moments are usually small. Someone who was stuck gets their code to run, or returns the following week with a better question. I have become more comfortable with pauses and with letting students show me what they have tried before I jump in. That makes the conversation easier for both of us.
