**Monday.** I made a list that would have suited three people. At the time it looked reasonable. I had slept well, the desk was clear, and I was apparently convinced that every task would take exactly as long as its shortest possible version.

By lunchtime, the first item had developed several smaller items. I added them underneath in smaller handwriting, as though that would make them less troublesome.

**Wednesday, I think.** I found myself rereading a paragraph without taking anything in. This is a very particular kind of tiredness: I can understand every word and still have no idea what the paragraph says. I made tea, forgot about it, and discovered it later when it was cold.

There is no dramatic explanation. I was having a slow week and did not like admitting it.

**Thursday evening.** Someone asked how things were going. I said “fine,” then immediately complained for about ten minutes. So, a fairly expansive definition of fine.

I had been comparing my untidy working notes with other people's finished work, which is a reliable way to spoil an afternoon. I knew what I was doing and continued doing it. Knowing better and feeling better were separate events that day.

---

On Friday I crossed out two things on the list without completing them. One could wait; the other had never really needed to be there. It felt slightly fraudulent and then quite relieving.

I also finally asked about a point I had been avoiding. The conversation helped, although it did not produce a magical answer or make me suddenly enthusiastic about everything. I had one useful next step and was still in a bad mood. Both were true.

**Sunday.** Laundry. A long phone call home. I bought groceries and discovered, after unpacking them, that I had forgotten the one thing I went out for. I decided it could wait until Monday.

I cooked enough for two meals, ate slightly more than one meal's share, and left the washing-up for a while. Later I put on music and did it. The kitchen looked better afterward, which was a modest but visible improvement.

I have not caught up with the list. Next week's page is blank for now. I am enjoying it that way for another evening.
